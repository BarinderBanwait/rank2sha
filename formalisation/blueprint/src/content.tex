\chapter{Introduction}

This blueprint tracks the Lean~4 formalisation (library
\texttt{FinShaRank2}, Mathlib \texttt{v4.33.1}) of the paper
\emph{Second derivatives of $p$-adic $L$-functions and the
Shafarevich--Tate group of rank-two CM elliptic curves}. The curve
throughout is $E : y^2 = x^3 - 56x$ over $\Q$: CM by $\Z[i]$, conductor
$N = 12544$, rank $2$, torsion $\Z/2$, $\prod_v c_v = 4$, root number
$w(E) = +1$; the primes are the split primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$.

Theorem~A of the paper (Theorem~3.8) is the node \emph{TheoremA}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:theoremA}). Theorem~B (Theorem~6.7) has no Lean
counterpart; the arithmetic half of its equivalence is the node
\emph{Dictionary} (Chapter~\ref{ch:dictionary}).

\section{What is machine-checked, and what is assumed}

Mathlib has no Selmer group, no Shafarevich--Tate group and no $p$-adic
$L$-function. The formalisation therefore assumes the classical theorems the
paper takes from the literature, as fields of one structure,
\texttt{ClassicalInputs}, and machine-checks everything the paper deduces
from them. Every theorem takes \texttt{H : ClassicalInputs} as an ordinary
hypothesis. There is no \texttt{axiom} declaration in the project and no
conjectural hypothesis.

Every declaration marked \texttt{\textbackslash leanok} in a proof below is
sorry-free, uses no \texttt{native\_decide}, and is confirmed by
\texttt{FinShaRank2/AxiomAudit.lean} to depend on no axiom beyond
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The
derivation of the two theorems from the classical inputs has also been
checked with Lean's \texttt{comparator}, as the README of the formalisation
records.

\section{Reading the graph}
\label{sec:reading}

There are three kinds of node.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Classical inputs} (Chapter~\ref{ch:inputs}), in the
    environment \emph{Classical input}. Each is a theorem of the literature,
    or a standing hypothesis of the paper, stated in Lean as a field of the
    assumption surface. Each node names the field and the source. None has
    a proof in the project, and none will: they are cited. In the graph they
    have a green border, which means the statement is in Lean, and no green
    body, which means no proof is.
  \item \textbf{Kernel lemmas} (Chapter~\ref{ch:kernel}) and the two
    \textbf{theorems} (Chapters~\ref{ch:theoremA} and~\ref{ch:dictionary}).
    Each is proved in Lean, and its node has a green body. A node is dark
    green when everything it depends on is proved, and light green when
    something it depends on is a classical input. The theorems are light
    green: that is the statement ``proved modulo the literature'', made
    visible.
  \item \textbf{Definitions} (Chapter~\ref{ch:definitions}), drawn as
    boxes.
\end{itemize}

A dashed arrow from $A$ to $B$ means the Lean proof of $B$ consumes $A$. The
arrows into \emph{TheoremA} are the paper's Steps 1--5 in the order the
proof takes them, and the classical inputs among them are exactly the
theorems the paper cites there: Mazur--Tate--Teitelbaum, Rubin, Greenberg,
Mazur.

Material the paper contains but the formalisation does not attempt is
listed in Chapter~\ref{ch:descoped} with no theorem-like environment, so it
cannot be mistaken for an oversight.

\chapter{Definitions}
\label{ch:definitions}

\begin{definition}[The normalised second jet]
  \label{def:NormalisedSecondJet}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.c2tilde, FinShaRank2.PrimeData.c2tilde}
  \leanok
  For $p$ split with $\alpha_p$ the unit root of $X^2 - a_pX + p$, and
  $L_p(E,T) = c_0(p) + c_1(p)\,T + c_2(p)\,T^2 + \cdots$, set
  \[
     \tilde c_2(p) \;:=\; c_2(p) \cdot (1 - \alpha_p^{-1})^{-2} \cdot
     \frac{(\#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}})^2}{\prod_v c_v} .
  \]
  This is Definition~3.2 of the paper.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{c2tilde} is a total function of its three numerical arguments,
  matching the display. At an anomalous prime $1 - \alpha_p^{-1} = 0$ and the
  value is a junk value; every theorem about \texttt{c2tilde} therefore
  carries the non-anomalous hypothesis explicitly.
  \texttt{PrimeData.c2tilde} applies it to the $L$-function, unit root and
  rational factor of a per-prime bundle.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Iwasawa invariants of a power series]
  \label{def:IwasawaInvariants}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.MuZero, FinShaRank2.lambdaAn}
  \leanok
  For $f \in \Lambda = \Z_p[[T]]$, $\mu(f) = 0$ means some coefficient of
  $f$ is a $p$-adic unit, and $\lambda(f)$ is the least index of a unit
  coefficient.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{MuZero f} is $\exists n,\ \mathrm{IsUnit}(\mathrm{coeff}\ n\ f)$
  and \texttt{lambdaAn f} is the infimum of that set of indices, with the
  convention $\inf \varnothing = 0$. The two are used together: $\lambda$
  carries information only where $\mu = 0$.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The classical inputs}
\label{ch:inputs}

Each node of this chapter is a field of the assumption surface, the six
files under \texttt{FinShaRank2/Interface/}. Its statement is the field's
type; its source is the theorem of the literature the field renders, or the
hypothesis of the paper it records. The nodes are grouped by the layer of
the interface that carries them. Fields that no theorem of the project
consumes are not nodes, with one exception noted at the end.

\section{The $p$-adic $L$-function}

\begin{assumption}[Interpolation at the trivial character]
  \label{assum:Interpolation}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.AnalyticData.interp}
  \leanok
  For a good ordinary prime $p$ with unit root $\alpha_p$,
  \[
    L_p(E,0) \;=\; (1 - \alpha_p^{-1})^2\, \frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E} .
  \]

  Cited from Mazur--Tate--Teitelbaum, Invent.\ Math.\ 84 (1986), 1--48. In
  Lean the field \texttt{AnalyticData.interp} states the identity in $\Q_p$
  between the constant coefficient of $L_p$ and the modular symbol.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Vanishing of the central value]
  \label{assum:CentralValueZero}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.AnalyticData.msymb_zero}
  \leanok
  $L(E,1)/\Omega_E = 0$.

  This is the hypothesis $L(E,1) = 0$ of the paper's theorems. For the curve
  $y^2 = x^3 - 56x$ it is an exact computation on modular symbols (Stein--Wuthrich,
  Math.\ Comp.\ 82 (2013), \S 12), and it follows from
  $\rank E(\Q) = 2$ and the theorem of Gross--Zagier and Kolyvagin. In Lean
  it is the field \texttt{AnalyticData.msymb\_zero}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[The $p$-adic functional equation]
  \label{assum:FunctionalEquation}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.AnalyticData.funct_eq}
  \leanok
  With $w(E) = +1$, there is a unit $U \in \Lambda^\times$ with $U(0) = 1$
  and
  \[
    L_p\bigl(E, (1+T)^{-1} - 1\bigr) \;=\; U(T)\, L_p(E,T) .
  \]

  Cited from Mazur--Tate--Teitelbaum, op.\ cit. In Lean the field
  \texttt{AnalyticData.funct\_eq} states the existence of $U$, with the
  substitution $(1+T)^{-1} - 1$ rendered as \texttt{Lp.subst} $\sigma$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[The unit root]
  \label{assum:UnitRoot}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.AnalyticData.alpha_root}
  \leanok
  At a good ordinary prime, $X^2 - a_pX + p$ has a root
  $\alpha_p \in \Z_p^\times$.

  Hensel's lemma at a good ordinary prime. In Lean the field
  \texttt{AnalyticData.alpha\_root} states the equation for the unit
  $\alpha$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[The Hasse bound]
  \label{assum:HasseBound}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.AnalyticData.hasse}
  \leanok
  $a_p^2 \le 4p$.

  Hasse's theorem. In Lean the field \texttt{AnalyticData.hasse}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[{The Frobenius trace of a curve with CM by $\Z[i]$}]
  \label{assum:DeuringTrace}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.AnalyticData.ap_from_CM}
  \leanok
  For $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ there is $\pi \in \Z[i]$ with $\pi\bar\pi = p$
  and $a_p = \pi + \bar\pi = 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\pi)$.

  Deuring's theorem. In Lean the field \texttt{AnalyticData.ap\_from\_CM},
  with $\pi$ a Gaussian integer of norm $p$.
\end{assumption}

\section{The standing hypotheses of the paper}

\begin{assumption}[The excluded set and the rational factor]
  \label{assum:StandingHypotheses}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ClassicalInputs.notAnomalous, FinShaRank2.ClassicalInputs.torsSqOverTam_eq}
  \leanok
  For every split prime $p \notin S_E$, $a_p \not\equiv 1 \pmod p$; and
  $(\#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}})^2 / \prod_v c_v = 1$.

  The first is the clause $S_{\mathrm{an}} \subseteq S_E$ of the paper's
  definition~(8) of the excluded set. The second is the arithmetic of
  Table~1: $2^2/4 = 1$. In Lean the fields
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.notAnomalous} and
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.torsSqOverTam\_eq}. What the first assumes beyond
  the proved Lemma~\ref{lem:NoAnomalousPrimes} is the single value
  $a_5 \ne -4$.
\end{assumption}

\section{Iwasawa theory and descent}

\begin{assumption}[The structure of the Selmer dual and Rubin's main conjecture]
  \label{assum:RubinStructure}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.IwasawaData.rubin_structure}
  \leanok
  Let $X = \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q_\infty)^\vee$. There are $n$,
  elements $f_1, \dots, f_n \in \Lambda$ and a $\Lambda$-linear map
  $X \to \prod_i \Lambda/(f_i)$ with finite kernel and cokernel, such that
  $\prod_i f_i$ and $L_p(E,T)$ generate the same ideal.

  The pseudo-isomorphism is the structure theorem for finitely generated
  $\Lambda$-modules, Washington, \emph{Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields},
  2nd ed., Thm.~13.12. The identification of the characteristic ideal with
  $(L_p)$ is Rubin, Invent.\ Math.\ 103 (1991), Thm.~12.3, through Yager's
  identification of the two-variable characteristic ideal with the Katz
  measure restricted to the cyclotomic line. In Lean the field
  \texttt{IwasawaData.rubin\_structure} states the conjunction.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[No finite submodule]
  \label{assum:NoFiniteSubmodule}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.IwasawaData.no_finite_submodule}
  \leanok
  $X = \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q_\infty)^\vee$ has no nonzero finite
  $\Lambda$-submodule.

  Greenberg, \emph{Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves}, LNM 1716 (1999),
  Prop.~4.14, which applies because $X$ is $\Lambda$-torsion and
  $E(\Q)[p] = 0$. In Lean the field
  \texttt{IwasawaData.no\_finite\_submodule}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Mazur's control theorem]
  \label{assum:MazurControl}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.IwasawaData.control}
  \leanok
  $X/TX \cong \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q)^\vee$ as $\Z_p$-modules.

  Mazur, Invent.\ Math.\ 18 (1972), in the form of an isomorphism, which is
  what the hypotheses of Theorem~A give: $p$ is a good ordinary prime,
  $E(\Q)[p] = 0$, and $p \nmid \prod_v c_v$. In Lean the field
  \texttt{IwasawaData.control} is the $\Z_p$-linear equivalence.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[The dual descent sequence]
  \label{assum:DescentSequence}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.SelmerData.mw_sha_exact, FinShaRank2.SelmerData.π_surj, FinShaRank2.SelmerData.ι_inj}
  \leanok
  With $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, the Pontryagin dual of
  $0 \to E(\Q) \otimes \Q_p/\Z_p \to \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q) \to
  \Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] \to 0$ is an exact sequence of $\Z_p$-modules
  \[
    0 \to \Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]^\vee \xrightarrow{\ \iota\ }
    \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q)^\vee \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } \Z_p^2 \to 0 .
  \]

  Exactness is Pontryagin duality applied to the descent sequence; the
  target $\Z_p^2$ is the rank, which for $y^2 = x^3 - 56x$ is a $2$-descent
  with saturation (\S 7.1 of the paper). In Lean the fields
  \texttt{SelmerData.}$\iota$\texttt{\_inj}, \texttt{SelmerData.}$\pi$\texttt{\_surj} and
  \texttt{SelmerData.mw\_sha\_exact}.
\end{assumption}

\section{$p$-adic heights}

\begin{assumption}[The leading term of $L_p$]
  \label{assum:PadicBSDLeadingTerm}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.HeightData.spr_padicBSD, FinShaRank2.HeightData.spr_nondeg}
  \leanok
  For a split prime $p$ with $\prod_v c_v/(\#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}})^2 = 1$,
  \[
    \|\tilde c_2(p)\| \;=\; \|\Reg_\gamma\| \cdot \|\#\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]\| ,
  \]
  and $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$ if and only if the cyclotomic $p$-adic
  height pairing on $E(\Q)$ is nondegenerate, $v_p(\Reg_\gamma) = 0$, and
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$. This is display~(7) of the paper.

  Schneider, Invent.\ Math.\ 79 (1985), Thms.~2 and~2$'$, and Perrin-Riou,
  Invent.\ Math.\ 109 (1992), \S\S 3.4.2--3.4.3, in the form of
  Stein--Wuthrich, Math.\ Comp.\ 82 (2013), Thm.~6.1, under Rubin's main
  conjecture. In Lean the fields \texttt{HeightData.spr\_padicBSD} (the norm
  identity) and \texttt{HeightData.spr\_nondeg} (the equivalence), both
  stated for the height layer's own values \texttt{c2norm} and
  \texttt{shaOrd}; the next node ties those values to the analytic jet and to
  $\Sha$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Identification of the height-side values]
  \label{assum:ProxyTies}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.PrimeData.c2norm_tie, FinShaRank2.PrimeData.shaOrd_tie}
  \leanok
  The value \texttt{c2norm} of the height layer is $\tilde c_2(p)$, and
  \texttt{shaOrd} is a $p$-adic unit if and only if
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$.

  These are not theorems of the literature. They are the two equations that
  weld the height layer to the analytic and Selmer layers, and the
  Dictionary depends on them. In Lean the fields
  \texttt{PrimeData.c2norm\_tie} and \texttt{PrimeData.shaOrd\_tie}. The
  height layer keeps its own values so that the witness of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:AssumptionsDoNotForceShaZero} can be constructed.
\end{assumption}

\section{The Katz measure}

\begin{assumption}[Comparison of $L_p$ with the Katz branch]
  \label{assum:KatzComparison}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.KatzData.comparison}
  \leanok
  Let $L^{\mathrm{Katz}}_\fp(T)$ be the restriction of the Katz measure
  $\mu_{\ff\fp^\infty}$ to the cyclotomic line through the central character
  of $\psi_E$. Then
  \[
    L_p(E,T) = c_p \cdot u(T) \cdot L^{\mathrm{Katz}}_\fp(T)
  \]
  for some $c_p \in \overline{\Q}_p^\times$ and some unit power series
  $u(T)$ over the ring of integers of a finite extension of $\Q_p$, and
  $v_p(c_p) = 0$ for every split $p \notin S_{\mathrm{cmp}}$, where
  $S_{\mathrm{cmp}} := \{p : p \mid 6N\,\Nm\ff\} \cup
  \operatorname{supp}(\omega_E)$ and $\omega_E$ is the period ratio. This is
  Lemma~3.5 of the paper.

  Bannai--Kobayashi, Duke Math.\ J.\ 153 (2010), Cor.~3.12, with de~Shalit,
  \emph{Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves with complex multiplication}
  (1987), II \S 4, for the local terms. In Lean the field
  \texttt{KatzData.comparison}: after base change to the coefficient ring
  $W$, $L_p = c \cdot u(T) \cdot L^{\mathrm{Katz}}$ with $c \in W^\times$ and
  $u$ a unit series. The conclusion $v_p(c_p) = 0$ is part of the field's
  type, for the primes $S_{\mathrm{cmp}}$ does not exclude; for
  $y^2 = x^3 - 56x$ the period ratio is supported on $\{2, 7\}$, so
  $S_{\mathrm{cmp}}$ contains no split prime.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[The grading congruence for the second moment]
  \label{assum:GradingCongruence}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.KatzData.grading_congr}
  \leanok
  Let $L^{\mathrm{K}}(T) \in W[[T]]$ be the Katz branch of the previous node
  and $M_2(\fp)$ the second moment of \S 6.1 of the paper. Then
  \[
    2\log_p(1+p)^2\, c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}}) \equiv M_2(\fp) \pmod{p^3 W} .
  \]
  This is Proposition~6.2(1) of the paper.

  Bannai--Kobayashi, op.\ cit., Prop.~3.3 and Thm.~3.7, with de~Shalit II
  \S 4. The integral formula for $c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}})$ as a second moment
  (Proposition~6.1 of the paper) is not carried in Lean; only this
  congruence is, as the field \texttt{KatzData.grading\_congr}:
  $\exists\, \kappa_0 \in W^\times,\ p^2\bigl(\kappa_0 \cdot
  \mathrm{coeff}_2\,L^{\mathrm{Katz}} - \texttt{m2core}\bigr) \in (p^3)$.
\end{assumption}

\section{The Eisenstein--Kronecker data}

\begin{assumption}[The divisor and the class functions]
  \label{assum:ClassFunctions}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.EKPackage}
  \leanok
  Let $\ff$ be the conductor of $\psi_E$, $\varepsilon$ its unit character,
  $f_0$ a generator of $\ff$, and for $g \in (\OK/\ff)^\times$ put
  $t_g := g\,\Omega_\infty/f_0$. The divisor is
  $D_E := \mathrm{Cl}_\ff(K) = (\OK/\ff)^\times/\mu_K$, and for $a \ge 0$,
  $b \ge 1$ the class function $r_{a,b}$ on $D_E$ is
  \[
    r_{a,b} \colon [g] \longmapsto
      \varepsilon(g)^{-(a+b)}\, \frac{e^*_{a,b}(t_g, 0)}{A(\Gamma)^a}
      \;\in\; \Qbar .
  \]
  This is Definition~5.1 of the paper.

  Bannai--Kobayashi, op.\ cit., (15) and Thm.~1.17 for the coefficients
  $e^*_{a,b}$, Thm.~2.9 and Cor.~2.11 for algebraicity. In Lean the structure
  \texttt{EKPackage}: a coefficient field \texttt{K}, a value field
  \texttt{L}, a finite nonempty set \texttt{D} of points, six functions
  \texttt{r} at the indices with $a + b \le 3$, and a valuation on
  \texttt{L} at each rational prime. The truncation to six indices is the
  formalisation's own.

  This is the exception announced above: no theorem of the project reads a
  value of \texttt{r}. The class functions enter the paper through the
  analytic half of Theorem~B, which is not formalised
  (Chapter~\ref{ch:descoped}). The node is kept so that the assumption
  surface shown here is the whole of \texttt{ClassicalInputs}.
\end{assumption}

\chapter{Kernel lemmas}
\label{ch:kernel}

The lemmas of this chapter are proved in Lean. Some are pure algebra with no
classical input behind them; those are dark green in the graph. The others
consume classical inputs and are light green.

\begin{lemma}[Linear coefficient under the functional equation]
  \label{lem:FunctionalEquationCoefficient}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.coeff_one_eq_zero_of_functionalEquation, FinShaRank2.coeff_one_Lp_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  Let $R$ be a commutative ring in which $2x = 0$ implies $x = 0$, let
  $f, U \in R[[T]]$ with $f(0) = 0$ and $U(0) = 1$, and suppose
  $f\bigl((1+T)^{-1} - 1\bigr) = U\,f$. Then the coefficient of $T$ in $f$ is
  $0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Comparing coefficients of $T$ on both sides gives $-c_1 = c_1$, so
  $2c_1 = 0$. \texttt{coeff\_one\_Lp\_eq\_zero} is the specialisation to
  $R = \Z_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Second-order vanishing at the trivial character]
  \label{lem:VanishingC0C1}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.c0_eq_zero, FinShaRank2.c1_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{assum:Interpolation, assum:CentralValueZero, assum:FunctionalEquation, lem:FunctionalEquationCoefficient}
  Assume $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$. Then for every good ordinary prime
  $p$, writing $L_p(E,T) = c_0(p) + c_1(p)\,T + c_2(p)\,T^2 + \cdots$,
  \[
    c_0(p) = c_1(p) = 0 .
  \]
  This is Lemma~3.1 of the paper.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  $c_0(p) = 0$ (\texttt{c0\_eq\_zero}): the interpolation formula with
  $L(E,1)/\Omega_E = 0$ gives $c_0(p) = 0$ in $\Q_p$, hence in $\Z_p$.
  $c_1(p) = 0$ (\texttt{c1\_eq\_zero}): the functional equation and
  $c_0(p) = 0$ put $L_p$ under
  Lemma~\ref{lem:FunctionalEquationCoefficient}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[{Anomalous primes of a curve with CM by $\Z[i]$}]
  \label{lem:NoAnomalousPrimes}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.anomalous_iff_five, FinShaRank2.noAnomalous, FinShaRank2.eq_five_or_thirteen_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{assum:HasseBound, assum:DeuringTrace, assum:UnitRoot}
  Let $E/\Q$ have complex multiplication by $\Z[i]$ and let
  $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ be a prime of good reduction. Then $a_p$ is even,
  and $p$ is anomalous for $E$ if and only if $p = 5$ and $a_5 = -4$.
  This is Lemma~2.2 of the paper, for split $p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{anomalous\_iff\_five}: over $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $2 \mid a$ and
  $a^2 \le 4p$, $(a : \Z/p) = 1$ if and only if $p = 5$ and $a = -4$.
  \texttt{eq\_five\_or\_thirteen\_le} splits into $p = 5$ and $p \ge 13$;
  for $p \ge 13$ the Hasse bound excludes $a \equiv 1$, and at $p = 5$ the
  congruence, the bound and evenness leave $a = -4$. Evenness is
  \texttt{two\_dvd\_ap}, from $a_p = 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\pi)$.
  \texttt{noAnomalous} is the form the normalisation consumes: for split
  $p$ with $a_5 \ne -4$ when $p = 5$, $1 - \alpha_p^{-1}$ is a $p$-adic
  unit. The part of the paper's lemma for $p \not\equiv 1 \pmod 4$ is not
  formalised; no theorem uses it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Normalisation]
  \label{prop:Normalisation}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.isPUnit_c2tilde_iff, FinShaRank2.isPUnit_c2tilde_iff_of_split, FinShaRank2.ClassicalInputs.isPUnit_one_sub_alphaInv}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:NormalisedSecondJet, lem:NoAnomalousPrimes, assum:StandingHypotheses}
  Let $p = \fp\bar\fp$ be a split prime of good reduction with
  $p \nmid \prod_v c_v \cdot \#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, not anomalous. Then
  $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$ if and only if $c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$.
  This is Proposition~3.3 of the paper.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{isPUnit\_c2tilde\_iff} proves the equivalence from two
  hypotheses: $1 - \alpha_p^{-1}$ is a $p$-adic unit, and
  $(\#\mathrm{tors})^2/\prod_v c_v = 1$. Theorem~A discharges the first
  through \texttt{ClassicalInputs.isPUnit\_one\_sub\_alphaInv}, from the
  field \texttt{notAnomalous}, and the second from
  \texttt{torsSqOverTam\_eq}. \texttt{isPUnit\_c2tilde\_iff\_of\_split}
  discharges the first instead from Lemma~\ref{lem:NoAnomalousPrimes}; it
  is kept because it bounds what \texttt{notAnomalous} assumes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A series with a double zero and unit second coefficient]
  \label{lem:TSquaredUnit}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.tsq_factor, FinShaRank2.muZero_of_coeffs, FinShaRank2.lambdaAn_eq_two_of_coeffs, FinShaRank2.associated_X_sq_of_coeffs}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:IwasawaInvariants}
  Let $f \in \Lambda$ with $c_0(f) = c_1(f) = 0$ and $c_2(f) \in
  \Z_p^\times$. Then $f = T^2 u$ for a unit $u \in \Lambda^\times$; hence
  $\mu(f) = 0$, $\lambda(f) = 2$, and $f$ and $T^2$ generate the same
  ideal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{tsq\_factor} produces $u$; the three consequences are read off
  from the factorisation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Selmer dual is free of rank two]
  \label{lem:SelmerDualStructure}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.selmer_dual_structure, FinShaRank2.quotient_collapse, FinShaRank2.rank_lower_bound}
  \leanok
  Let $X$ be a finitely generated $\Lambda$-module with no nonzero finite
  submodule, and suppose there is a $\Lambda$-linear map
  $X \to \prod_i \Lambda/(f_i)$ with finite kernel and cokernel such that
  $\prod_i f_i$ and $T^2$ generate the same ideal. If
  $\dim_{\Q_p}(X/TX) \otimes \Q_p \ge 2$, then $TX = 0$ and $X \cong \Z_p^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{selmer\_dual\_structure} is the statement. The pseudo-isomorphism
  and the characteristic ideal $(T^2)$ bound the $\Z_p$-rank of $X/TX$ by
  $2$; with the lower bound, $X/TX$ is free of rank $2$, and the absence of
  finite submodules forces $TX = 0$. \texttt{rank\_lower\_bound} supplies
  the lower bound from a surjection onto $\Z_p^2$, and
  \texttt{quotient\_collapse} identifies $X/TX$ with $X$ once $TX = 0$.
  This is the algebraic core of Theorem~A, Steps 2 and 3 of the paper.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The descent endgame]
  \label{lem:ShaEndgame}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.sha_endgame, FinShaRank2.sha_endgame_of_nonempty}
  \leanok
  Let $0 \to A \xrightarrow{\iota} S \xrightarrow{\pi} \Z_p^2 \to 0$ be an
  exact sequence of finitely generated $\Z_p$-modules with $S \cong \Z_p^2$.
  Then $A = 0$ and $\mathrm{rank}_{\Z_p} S = 2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  $\pi$ is a surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated module over a
  commutative ring, up to the identification $S \cong \Z_p^2$, hence
  injective; so $\ker \pi = \iota(A) = 0$. This is Step~5 of the paper.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Decoupling]
  \label{lem:Decoupling}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.Decoupling.coeff_two_mul, FinShaRank2.Decoupling.isUnit_coeff_two_of_comparison}
  \leanok
  \uses{assum:KatzComparison}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $A, B \in R[[T]]$ with
      $c_0(B) = c_1(B) = 0$. Then $c_2(AB) = A(0)\, c_2(B)$.
    \item For $p \notin S_E$ split, $c_2(p)$ is a $p$-adic unit if and only
      if $c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}})$ is.
  \end{enumerate}
  This is Lemma~6.3 of the paper.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Part (1) is \texttt{Decoupling.coeff\_two\_mul}: $c_2(AB) = c_0(A)c_2(B) +
  c_1(A)c_1(B) + c_2(A)c_0(B)$ and the last two terms vanish. Part (2) is
  \texttt{Decoupling.isUnit\_coeff\_two\_of\_comparison}: part (1) along a
  local ring homomorphism moves the unit property across the comparison of
  Classical input~\ref{assum:KatzComparison}. No derivative of the
  comparison factor or of the Euler factor reaches the second coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The criterion class]
  \label{prop:CriterionClass}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.isUnit_iff_residue_ne_zero_of_grading_congr}
  \leanok
  \uses{assum:GradingCongruence}
  Let $W$ be a local integral domain with maximal ideal $(\varpi)$, let
  $\kappa_0 \in W^\times$, and let $c, m \in W$ with
  $\varpi^2(\kappa_0 c - m) \in (\varpi^3)$. Then $c$ is a unit if and only
  if the residue of $m$ is nonzero. Applied to the grading congruence:
  $v_p(c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}})) = 0$ if and only if the criterion class
  $\mathfrak{c}(\fp) := p^{-2}M_2(\fp) \bmod \mathfrak{m}_W$ is nonzero.
  This is Proposition~6.2(2) of the paper.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  The Lean lemma is the abstract statement about $W$, with no interface
  dependence; it is instantiated at $\varpi = p$ over the Katz coefficient
  ring.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Theorem A}
\label{ch:theoremA}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem A]
  \label{thm:TheoremA}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.prop_consequence}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:NormalisedSecondJet, def:IwasawaInvariants, prop:Normalisation, lem:VanishingC0C1, lem:TSquaredUnit, lem:SelmerDualStructure, lem:ShaEndgame, assum:RubinStructure, assum:NoFiniteSubmodule, assum:MazurControl, assum:DescentSequence}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$. Let
  $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ be a split prime of good reduction with
  $p \notin S_E$ and $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mu_{\mathrm{an}}(\fp) = 0$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{an}}(\fp) = 2$;
    \item $\corank_{\Z_p}\, \Sel_{p^\infty}(E/\Q) = 2$;
    \item $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$.
  \end{enumerate}
  This is Theorem~3.8 of the paper.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{prop\_consequence} takes $H : \texttt{ClassicalInputs}$, a split
  prime $p \notin H.S$, and the unit condition at $p$, and concludes the
  four statements on the Pontryagin-dual side: \texttt{MuZero},
  \texttt{lambdaAn = 2}, \texttt{finrank SelDual = 2} and
  \texttt{Subsingleton ShaDual}. The curve-level hypotheses are carried by
  $H$ and not restated.

  The proof follows the paper's steps. Proposition~\ref{prop:Normalisation}
  turns the unit condition into $c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$.
  Lemma~\ref{lem:VanishingC0C1} gives $c_0 = c_1 = 0$.
  Lemma~\ref{lem:TSquaredUnit} gives $L_p = T^2 u$, hence (1), and the
  characteristic ideal $(T^2)$. Mazur's control theorem
  (\ref{assum:MazurControl}) and the surjection of the descent sequence
  (\ref{assum:DescentSequence}) give $\mathrm{rank}\,(X/TX) \ge 2$. Rubin's
  structure statement (\ref{assum:RubinStructure}) and the absence of finite
  submodules (\ref{assum:NoFiniteSubmodule}) feed
  Lemma~\ref{lem:SelmerDualStructure}, which gives
  $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q)^\vee \cong \Z_p^2$ through the control
  isomorphism. Lemma~\ref{lem:ShaEndgame} on the descent sequence gives (2)
  and (3).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Analytic--arithmetic dictionary]
  \label{prop:Dictionary}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.prop_dictionary}

  needed: this route never converts between $\tilde c_2$ and $c_2$.

\begin{theorem}[The assumption surface is satisfiable]
  \label{thm:AssumptionsSatisfiable}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ToyTrivial}
  \leanok
  There is an instance \texttt{ToyTrivial : ClassicalInputs}, with every
  field discharged in a toy world ($S = \varnothing$, $D_E$ a single point,

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok

  \texttt{\#print axioms ToyTrivial} shows only \texttt{propext},
  \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The assumption surface does not entail the conclusion]
  \label{thm:AssumptionsDoNotForceShaZero}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ToySha, FinShaRank2.interface_does_not_force_sha_trivial, FinShaRank2.toySha_conclusions_fail, FinShaRank2.toySha_fails_c2_5_certificate}
  \leanok
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs} does not by itself entail
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$: there is a second instance
  \texttt{ToySha : ClassicalInputs} at which $\Sha[p^\infty] \ne 0$,
  $\mu_{\mathrm{an}} \ne 0$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{an}} \ne 2$ at every split
  prime, and at which the computed $5$-adic expansion of $c_2(5)$ is false.
  The unit condition, not the interface, is what excludes this world.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{toySha\_conclusions\_fail} shows the three conclusions fail at
  every split prime of the world \texttt{ToySha};
  \texttt{interface\_does\_not\_force\_sha\_trivial} is the non-entailment
  statement. \texttt{toySha\_fails\_c2\_5\_certificate} shows that in this
  world $\mathrm{coeff}_2\, L_p = p$, so the leading $5$-adic digit of
  $c_2(5)$ is $0$, whereas the computed value for $y^2 = x^3 - 56x$ has
  leading digit $1$. The seven $5$-adic digits used there are this project's
  own computation, cross-checked by two implementations; the paper does not
  display them.
\end{proof}

The items below are content of the paper that the formalisation does not
attempt. None carries a \texttt{\textbackslash lean} tag, and none is a node
the proved theorems depend on.

\paragraph{Background (\S 2 of the paper).} The theorem of Gillard and
Sinnott that the $\mu$-invariant of every branch of the Katz measure
vanishes, and the facts about the Kronecker theta function recalled from
Bannai--Kobayashi. Expository; nothing downstream depends on them.

regulator at every split prime below $30{,}000$ for each of the five
curves, the chance model, and the evaluation of $B(\fp)$. The scripts and
their outputs are in \texttt{code/}, not in the Lean tree.

\paragraph{The Coates--Liang--Sujatha input (\S 7.3).} The vanishing of
$\Sha[p^\infty]$ for the five curves at every split $p < 30{,}000$, and its
transfer along the $2$-isogeny. Cited from the literature.
