\chapter{Introduction}

This blueprint tracks the Lean~4 formalization (library
\texttt{FinShaRank2}, mathlib pin \texttt{v4.33.1}) of the paper
\emph{Second derivatives of $p$-adic $L$-functions and the
Shafarevich--Tate group of rank-two CM elliptic curves}, for the testbed
curve $E : y^2 = x^3 - 56x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (CM by $\mathbb{Z}[i]$,
conductor $N = 12544$, rank $2$, torsion $\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\prod_v c_v = 4$,
root number $w(E) = +1$; split primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$).

The paper's three lettered results are all formalized. Theorem~A is
\texttt{cor:horizontal} (Chapter~\ref{ch:horizontal}), Theorem~B is
\texttt{prop:consequence} (Chapter~\ref{ch:consequence}), and Theorem~C is
\texttt{thm:reduction} (Chapter~\ref{ch:reduction}).

\section{What is machine-checked, and what is assumed}

The formalization machine-checks the paper's \emph{new} mathematics --- its
definitions, its deductions, and its full logical chain --- while treating
universally accepted classical theorems (Rubin, Mazur, Greenberg,
Mazur--Tate--Teitelbaum, Bannai--Kobayashi, de~Shalit,
Schneider/Perrin-Riou, Hasse) as explicitly documented, human-refereeable
\emph{hypotheses}: fields of the structure \texttt{ClassicalInputs}, never
global \texttt{axiom} declarations. There is a second, strictly smaller
category: the paper's conjectural inputs. \texttt{hyp:sinnott} and
\texttt{conj:EK} occur only as hypotheses of \texttt{thm\_reduction} and
\texttt{thm\_reduction\_of\_conjEK} and nowhere else; \texttt{conj:weak},
which is the paper's own hypothesis for Theorem~A, occurs only as a
hypothesis of \texttt{cor\_horizontal}. Neither \texttt{prop:consequence}
nor \texttt{prop:dictionary} depends on a conjecture.

A second structure \texttt{Certificates}, carrying this project's own
machine-verified numerics, was retired on 2026-08-29 together with the two
anchor corollaries it served (Chapter~\ref{ch:anchors}).

Every declaration marked \texttt{\textbackslash leanok} below is sorry-free, uses no
\texttt{native\_decide}, and --- for the project's audited declarations ---
has been confirmed by \texttt{FinShaRank2/AxiomAudit.lean} to depend on no
axiom outside $\{\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice},
\texttt{Quot.sound}\}$ (82 audited declarations, empty allowlist, verified
2026-08-29).
\textbf{A \texttt{\textbackslash leanok} mark is never applied to an assumption node}: those are
citations, not proof obligations, and marking them would misrepresent the
trust boundary this blueprint exists to make visible.

\section{Reading the graph}

Interface fields --- citable classical theorems, packaged as data the main
theorems consume as a hypothesis \texttt{H : ClassicalInputs} --- are
rendered in the \textbf{Assumption} environment, a distinct header word from
Lemma/Proposition/Theorem/Corollary/Definition. \texttt{src/web.tex} lists
\texttt{assumption} among the graph-visible kinds, so these nodes appear in
the auto-generated dependency graph; they are never marked
\texttt{\textbackslash leanok}, so they never receive the green fill a proved node gets.
Every such node carries its pinpoint citation, and is distinguished from a
Lean-kernel-proved result by header word and by fill colour alike. The
conjectural inputs \texttt{hyp:sinnott} and \texttt{conj:EK} are
additionally flagged \textbf{CONJECTURAL} in their statement text, and occur
only upstream of \texttt{thm:reduction} in the graph --- nothing downstream
of \texttt{prop:consequence} or \texttt{prop:dictionary} touches them.

Material the paper contains but this formalization deliberately does not
attempt (the analytic content of \texttt{prop:jetformula}'s integral
formula, the construction of the divisor $D_E$ and of the sections
$r_{a,b}$, the height-pairing analytics, the expository background of
\S\texttt{sec:mechanism}, the regulator scan of Part~2, the
Coates--Liang--Sujatha input, and the outlook) is listed in the closing
chapter with no theorem-like environment at all, so it cannot be mistaken
for an oversight.

\chapter{Foundational definitions}

\begin{definition}[Horizontal control]
  \label{def:horizontal}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.HorizontalControl}
  \leanok
  Let $E/\Q$ be an elliptic curve and let $\mathcal{P}$ be a set of rational
  primes. \emph{Horizontal control} holds for $E$ along $\mathcal{P}$ if
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p] = 0$ for all but finitely many $p \in \mathcal{P}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Rendered as a predicate on a set of primes \texttt{P} and a per-prime
  predicate \texttt{shaVanishes}: the set of \texttt{p \(\in\) P} at which
  vanishing fails is finite. This is a standalone definition, independent of
  the interface layers built later, matching the paper, where
  \texttt{def:horizontal} precedes all interface data.
  \texttt{cor:horizontal} (Chapter~\ref{ch:horizontal}) supplies the concrete
  predicate through the Selmer-dual layer, as \texttt{Subsingleton
  (\ldots).selmer.ShaDual}. That renders $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$, which is
  stronger than the $\Sha(E/\Q)[p] = 0$ the definition asks for, so what is
  proved implies \texttt{def:horizontal} and not conversely.

  The declaration was named \texttt{HorizontalVanishing} until 2026-08-29;
  ``horizontal control'' is the term paper v2 defines.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The normalised second jet]
  \label{def:c2tilde}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.c2tilde}
  \leanok
  For $p$ split with $\alpha_p$ the unit root of $X^2 - a_pX + p$, set
  \[
     \tilde c_2(p) \;:=\; c_2(p) \cdot (1 - \alpha_p^{-1})^{-2} \cdot
     \frac{(\#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}})^2}{\prod_v c_v} .
  \]
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  A standalone total function \texttt{c2tilde (c2 alphaInv : \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)) (torsSqOverTam : \(\mathbb{Q}\)) : \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)}
  of its three numerical arguments, matching the definition verbatim.
  \textbf{Junk-value convention}: at an anomalous prime $1 - \alpha_p^{-1} = 0$
  and the value degenerates; every theorem about \texttt{c2tilde} therefore
  carries the non-anomalous hypothesis explicitly (discharged for the
  testbed curve by \texttt{lem:noanomalous}).
\end{proof}

\chapter{Second-order vanishing at the trivial character}

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:c0c1}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.c0_eq_zero, FinShaRank2.c1_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  Assume $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$. Then for every good ordinary prime
  $p$, writing $L_p(E,T) = c_0(p) + c_1(p)\,T + c_2(p)\,T^2 + \cdots$,
  \[
    c_0(p) = c_1(p) = 0 .
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \textbf{$c_0(p) = 0$} (\texttt{c0\_eq\_zero}): the Mazur--Tate--Teitelbaum
  interpolation formula at the trivial character is carried as a
  data-equation, the interface field \texttt{AnalyticData.interp}:
  $\bigl((\mathrm{constantCoeff}\ L_p : \Z_p) : \Q_p\bigr) = (1 -
  \alpha_p^{-1})^2 \cdot (\text{modularSymbol0} : \Q_p)$. Combined with the
  certificate \texttt{AnalyticData.msymb\_zero} (the exact vanishing
  $L(E,1)/\Omega_E = 0$, computed by exact linear algebra on modular
  symbols), the right side collapses to $0$, and injectivity of
  $\Z_p \hookrightarrow \Q_p$ gives $c_0(p) = 0$ integrally.

  \textbf{$c_1(p) = 0$} (\texttt{c1\_eq\_zero}): the interface field
  \texttt{AnalyticData.funct\_eq} supplies the $p$-adic functional equation
  with $w(E) = +1$ already folded in --- a unit series
  $U \in \Lambda^\times$, $U(0)=1$, with $L_p(E,(1+T)^{-1}-1) = U(T)\,L_p(E,T)$.
  The ring-generic kernel lemma \texttt{FinShaRank2.coeff\_one\_Lp\_eq\_zero}
  (\texttt{Kernel/FunctionalEquation.lean}, mathlib-only, no interface
  dependence) compares linear
  coefficients of both sides abstractly, consuming $c_0(p) = 0$ from the
  first half, to conclude $-c_1(p) = c_1(p)$, hence $c_1(p) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The normalisation dictionary}

\begin{proposition}[Normalisation]
  \label{prop:normalisation}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.isPUnit_c2tilde_iff, FinShaRank2.isPUnit_c2tilde_iff_of_split}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:c2tilde, lem:noanomalous}
  Let $p = \fp\bar\fp$ be a split prime of good reduction with
  $p \nmid \prod_v c_v \cdot \#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, and suppose $p$ is not
  anomalous. Then
  \[
    v_\fp\bigl(\tilde c_2(p)\bigr) = v_\fp\bigl(c_2(p)\bigr) ;
  \]
  in particular $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$ if and only if
  $v_\fp(c_2(p)) = 0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Rendered as the equivalence, not as the equality of valuations:
  \texttt{isPUnit\_c2tilde\_iff} states $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times \iff
  c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$ from the two hypotheses that
  $1 - \alpha_p^{-1}$ is a $p$-adic unit and that the rational factor
  $(\#\mathrm{tors})^2/\prod_v c_v$ equals $1$ (the testbed value, pinned by
  the interface field \texttt{ClassicalInputs.torsSqOverTam\_eq}). It is
  ring-generic and carries no interface dependence.

  \texttt{isPUnit\_c2tilde\_iff\_of\_split} is the same statement with the
  unit hypothesis discharged from the \texttt{AnalyticData} fields
  \texttt{hasse}, \texttt{ap\_from\_CM} and \texttt{alpha\_root} through
  \texttt{noAnomalous} (Lemma~\ref{lem:noanomalous}). Since the interface
  field \texttt{ClassicalInputs.notAnomalous} was added on 2026-08-29 this
  composed form is no longer the route the main theorems take:
  \texttt{prop\_consequence} and \texttt{thm\_reduction} call
  \texttt{isPUnit\_c2tilde\_iff} with non-anomality supplied by
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.isPUnit\_one\_sub\_alphaInv}. It is kept audited
  because it bounds what \texttt{notAnomalous} assumes beyond the proved
  Lemma~\ref{lem:noanomalous}.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The horizontal rigidity conjectures}

\begin{conjecture}[Horizontal rigidity; strong form]
  \label{conj:strong}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ConjStrong}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:c2tilde, def:deltaE}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2 = \ord_{s=1} L(E,s)$. There exist a
  nonzero $\delta_E \in \Qbar^\times$, expressible through the
  Eisenstein--Kronecker numbers of the CM lattice of $E$, and a finite
  explicit set $\Sigma$ of primes, such that $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p$ for
  every split $p \notin \Sigma$, and $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$ for
  every split $p \notin \Sigma$ with $\fp \nmid \delta_E$.
\end{conjecture}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{ConjStrong H} is rendered as the existential the conjecture
  states: there are $\delta$ in \texttt{H.ek.L} --- the paper's $\Qbar$, the
  value field of the Eisenstein--Kronecker package
  (Assumption~\ref{eq:jetpackage}) --- and a \texttt{Finset} \texttt{Sig} of
  primes with $\delta \ne 0$ and,
  for every split $p \notin H.S$ with $p \notin \texttt{Sig}$ and
  $\fp \nmid \delta$, $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$. The paper's $\Sigma$
  is $H.S \cup \texttt{Sig}$: $H.S$ is the standing exclusion of every
  statement about $H$. \texttt{thm:reduction} witnesses the existential with
  $\delta := \texttt{H.ek.deltaE c}$ and $\texttt{Sig} := \texttt{H.ek.supp c}$,
  which is the paper's $\Sigma = S_E \cup \operatorname{supp}(c)$.

  Until 2026-08-29 the predicate pinned $\delta$ and $\Sigma$ to fields of
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs}, which discharged the existential by fiat.

  The divisibility condition is \texttt{H.ek.v p \(\delta\) = 1}, not a
  statement about a rational number: mathlib's \texttt{Valuation} is
  multiplicative, so the paper's $v_\fp(x) = 0$ reads as $v(x) = 1$ here.

  The integrality clause ``$\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p$'' is not part of the
  formal predicate. By \texttt{rmk:integrality} integrality follows from
  integrality of the modular symbols of $E$ and is classical; the paper
  records that it ``is therefore not its substance; the unit assertion is''.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Weak form]
  \label{conj:weak}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ConjWeak}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:c2tilde}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2 = \ord_{s=1} L(E,s)$. Then
  $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$ for all but finitely many split primes $p$
  of good reduction.
\end{conjecture}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Rendered as: there is a finite exceptional set $T$ such that every split
  $p \notin S$ outside $T$ has $\tilde c_2(p)$ a $p$-adic unit. This is the
  $\exists\ \text{finite } T,\ \forall p \notin T$-form that
  \texttt{cor:horizontal} (Chapter~\ref{ch:horizontal}) consumes.
  \texttt{prop:consequence} does not consume it: that proposition takes the
  unit condition at a single prime as a hypothesis and mentions no
  conjecture.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The Katz-measure comparison}

\begin{assumption}[Comparison of $L_p$ with the Katz branch]
  \label{lem:comparison}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.KatzData.comparison}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by $\OK$ and conductor $N$, let $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$
  be a good split prime, and let $L^{\mathrm{Katz}}_\fp(T)$ be the
  restriction of the Katz measure $\mu_{\ff\fp^\infty}$ to the cyclotomic
  line through the central character of $\psi_E$. Then
  \[
    L_p(E,T) = c_p \cdot u(T) \cdot L^{\mathrm{Katz}}_\fp(T)
  \]
  for some $c_p \in \overline{\Q}_p^\times$ and some unit power series
  $u(T)$ over the ring of integers of a finite extension of $\Q_p$.
  Moreover $v_p(c_p) = 0$ for every split $p \notin S_{\mathrm{cmp}}$, where
  $S_{\mathrm{cmp}} := \{p : p \mid 6N\,\Nm\ff\} \cup
  \operatorname{supp}(\omega_E)$ is finite, $\omega_E$ being the period
  ratio.

  \textbf{Classical input, not formalized as a theorem.} SOURCE:
  Bannai--Kobayashi, \emph{Algebraic theta functions and the $p$-adic
  interpolation of Eisenstein--Kronecker numbers}, Duke Math. J. 153 (2010),
  no. 2, 229--295, Cor. 3.12, together with de~Shalit, \emph{Iwasawa theory
  of elliptic curves with complex multiplication}, Perspectives in
  Mathematics 3 (1987), II \S4, for the local terms. Rendered in Lean as the
  interface field \texttt{KatzData.comparison}: after base change to the
  coefficient ring $W$, $L_p$ equals $c \cdot u(T) \cdot L^{\mathrm{Katz}}$
  for a \emph{unit} $c \in W^\times$ and a unit series $u$. The
  $v_p(c_p) = 0$ conclusion is baked into the field's type, for the primes
  $S_{\mathrm{cmp}}$ already excludes; for the testbed curve the period
  ratio is supported on $\{2,7\}$, so $S_{\mathrm{cmp}}$ excludes no split
  prime.
\end{assumption}

\chapter{Non-anomalous primes}

\begin{lemma}[{Anomalous primes of a curve with CM by $\Z[i]$}]
  \label{lem:noanomalous}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.anomalous_iff_five, FinShaRank2.noAnomalous, FinShaRank2.eq_five_or_thirteen_le}
  \leanok
  Let $E/\Q$ be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by $\Z[i]$ and
  let $p$ be a prime of good reduction for $E$. If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$,
  then $a_p$ is even, and $p$ is anomalous for $E$ if and only if $p = 5$
  and $a_5 = -4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{anomalous\_iff\_five} is the equivalence, over the hypotheses
  $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $2 \mid a$ and $a^2 \le 4p$: it proves
  $(a : \Z/p) = 1 \iff (p = 5 \wedge a = -4)$. Forward,
  \texttt{eq\_five\_or\_thirteen\_le} splits the split prime into $p = 5$ and
  $p \ge 13$; the second branch is killed by the Hasse-bound squeeze
  \texttt{ap\_ne\_one\_of\_hasse}, and at $p = 5$ the congruence, the Hasse
  bound and evenness leave only $a = -4$. Backward is
  $-4 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, by \texttt{decide}. Evenness of $a_p$ is
  \texttt{two\_dvd\_ap}, from the \texttt{AnalyticData} field
  \texttt{ap\_from\_CM} ($a_p = 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\pi)$ for a Gaussian integer
  $\pi$ of norm $p$).

  \texttt{noAnomalous} is the composed form the normalisation consumes: from
  \texttt{hasse}, \texttt{ap\_from\_CM}, \texttt{alpha\_root} and the case
  split $13 \le p \vee (p = 5 \wedge a_p = -2)$ it concludes that
  $1 - \alpha_p^{-1}$ is a $p$-adic unit. The testbed curve has $a_5 = -2$,
  so it is not the anomalous case.

  Part~(1) of the paper's lemma --- $p \not\equiv 1 \pmod 4$ implies
  $p \mid a_p$ --- is not formalized. Its proof reduces to Deuring's
  reduction criterion, and mathlib has neither that criterion nor the
  reduction of a CM elliptic curve. Every downstream theorem uses the split
  case only.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The arithmetic consequence of a unit second jet}
\label{ch:consequence}

\begin{theorem}[$=$ Theorem B]
  \label{prop:consequence}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.prop_consequence, FinShaRank2.ClassicalInputs.isPUnit_one_sub_alphaInv}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:c0c1, prop:normalisation, lem:noanomalous, def:c2tilde}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$. Let
  $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ be a split prime of good reduction with
  $p \notin S_E$ and $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mu_{\mathrm{an}}(\fp) = 0$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{an}}(\fp) = 2$;
    \item $\corank_{\Z_p}\, \Sel_{p^\infty}(E/\Q) = 2$;
    \item $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  The theorem's content is the implication from the unit condition at a
  \emph{single} prime, so the formal statement takes that condition as a
  hypothesis and mentions no conjecture. The curve-level hypotheses --- CM by
  the maximal order, $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$, $w(E) = +1$ --- are
  carried by $H : \texttt{ClassicalInputs}$ and its per-prime bundles, and
  are not restated in the Lean statement; this is the standing convention of
  the project. ``$p \ge 5$ of good reduction, $p \notin S_E$'' is
  \texttt{hsplit : p \% 4 = 1} together with \texttt{hpS : p \(\notin\) H.S}.
  \textbf{Assembly route}: (1)
  Proposition~\ref{prop:normalisation} turns the unit hypothesis into
  $c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$; (2) Lemma~\ref{lem:c0c1} gives $c_0=c_1=0$; (3)
  the ring-generic $T^2$-factorisation kernel
  (\texttt{Kernel/TsqUnit.lean}) turns $c_0=c_1=0$,
  $c_2 \in \Z_p^\times$ into $L_p = X^2 \cdot (\text{unit})$, i.e. $\mu=0$,
  $\lambda=2$; (4) Mazur's control theorem (interface field
  \texttt{IwasawaData.control}) transports the resulting rank bound onto the
  Selmer dual's coinvariants; (5) Rubin's main conjecture for CM elliptic
  curves, via the structure theorem for finitely generated
  $\Lambda$-modules (interface field \texttt{IwasawaData.rubin\_structure}),
  identifies the characteristic ideal with $(L_p)$ and feeds
  \texttt{FinShaRank2.selmer\_dual\_structure} (the project's
  highest-risk kernel lemma, \texttt{Kernel/LambdaModule.lean}) to pin $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q)^\vee
  \simeq \Z_p^2$; (6) the Pontryagin dual of the Mordell--Weil/$\Sha$ descent
  sequence (interface \texttt{SelmerData}, via
  \texttt{FinShaRank2.sha\_endgame\_of\_nonempty},
  \texttt{Kernel/ShaEndgame.lean}) then delivers
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]=0$ and the corank $2$ conclusion.

  \textbf{Where non-anomality comes from.} Until 2026-08-29 the exclusion
  $p \notin S_E$ carried no formal content --- $S$ was opaque
  \texttt{Finset} data --- and this theorem carried the residual hypothesis
  $p = 5 \Rightarrow a_p = -2$ in its place. The interface field
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.notAnomalous} now renders the $S_{\mathrm{an}}$
  clause of the paper's definition of $S_E$, as $a_p \not\equiv 1 \pmod p$
  for every split $p \notin S$, and the residual hypothesis is gone. The
  bridge from that field to Proposition~\ref{prop:normalisation} is
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.isPUnit\_one\_sub\_alphaInv}, which feeds it
  through \texttt{isPUnit\_one\_sub\_alphaInv\_iff}. Lemma~\ref{lem:noanomalous}
  proves the clause outright at every split $p \ge 13$ and reduces it at
  $p = 5$ to $a_5 \ne -4$, so what \texttt{notAnomalous} assumes beyond the
  proved lemma is a single numerical value.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[No finite submodule]
  \label{rmk:nofinitesub}
  The compact Selmer dual $X = \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q_\infty)^\vee$ has
  no nonzero finite $\Lambda$-submodule --- the hypothesis that lets Mazur's
  control theorem produce an honest isomorphism onto
  $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q)^\vee$ rather than a map with kernel/cokernel.

  \textbf{Classical input, not a separately labeled paper statement}
  (recorded in the running commentary of \texttt{prop:consequence}'s proof).
  SOURCE: Greenberg, \emph{Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves}, LNM 1716
  (Springer, 1999), Prop.~4.14, applicable here because
  $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/\Q_\infty)$ is $\Lambda$-cotorsion (Step 2,
  $\mathrm{car}_\Lambda(X) = (L_p) = (T^2)$) and $E(\Q)[p]=0$. Rendered as
  the interface field \texttt{IwasawaData.no\_finite\_submodule}.
\end{assumption}

\chapter{Horizontal control along the split primes}
\label{ch:horizontal}

\begin{corollary}[$=$ Theorem A]
  \label{cor:horizontal}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.cor_horizontal}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:horizontal, conj:weak, prop:consequence}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$ and with
  finitely many anomalous split primes, and assume
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:weak}. Then horizontal control
  (Definition~\ref{def:horizontal}) holds for $E$ along the split primes of
  $K$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{ConjWeak H} supplies a finite set $T$ such that every split
  $p \notin H.S$ outside $T$ has $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$;
  Theorem~\ref{prop:consequence} turns that into
  \texttt{Subsingleton (\ldots).selmer.ShaDual} at each such $p$. The set of
  split primes at which the per-prime predicate fails is therefore contained
  in $T$, which is finite. At $p \in H.S$ the predicate holds vacuously,
  there being no proof of $p \notin H.S$ to supply it, so $H.S$ does not
  enter the bound.

  ``The split primes of $K$'' is $\{p \mid p \text{ prime},\ p \equiv 1
  \pmod 4\}$; throughout the project $K = \Q(i)$.

  \textbf{Two deviations from the paper.} The predicate supplied is
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$ where \texttt{def:horizontal} asks for
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p] = 0$, so the conclusion proved is stronger than the one
  stated. And the paper derives finiteness of $S_E$ from its hypothesis that
  there are finitely many anomalous split primes, whereas
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.S} is a \texttt{Finset} and finite by
  construction; neither that hypothesis nor the first step of the paper's
  proof has a counterpart here. This is a simplification the encoding makes,
  not something proved.

  \texttt{hweak} is the paper's own hypothesis for this corollary and is the
  only conjectural hypothesis of the statement: \texttt{ConjEK} and
  \texttt{SinnottHyp} do not appear.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The height dictionary}

\begin{assumption}[$p$-adic BSD, leading-term form]
  \label{eq:padicbsd}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.HeightData.spr_padicBSD, FinShaRank2.HeightData.spr_nondeg}
  Let $p$ be a split prime with $\prod_v c_v/(\#E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}})^2 = 1$.
  The expected leading-coefficient identity reads
  \[
     c_2(p) \;=\; (1-\alpha_p^{-1})^2 \cdot \frac{\mathrm{Reg}_p}{\log_p(1+p)^2}
     \cdot \#\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]
  \]
  up to a $p$-adic unit, equivalently
  $\|\tilde c_2(p)\| = \|\mathrm{Reg}_\gamma\| \cdot \|\#\Sha[p^\infty]\|$,
  and (leading-term dictionary) $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times \iff$
  (the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing is nondegenerate,
  $v_p(\mathrm{Reg}_\gamma)=0$, and $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty]=0$).

  \textbf{Classical input, not formalized as a theorem.} SOURCE: Schneider,
  \emph{$p$-adic height pairings II}, Invent. Math. 79 (1985), 329--374, and
  Perrin-Riou, \emph{Théorie d'Iwasawa et hauteurs $p$-adiques}, Invent.
  Math. 109 (1992), 137--185, packaged as Stein--Wuthrich, \emph{Algorithms
  for the arithmetic of elliptic curves using Iwasawa theory}, Math. Comp.
  82 (2013), 1757--1792, Thm.~6.1. Rendered as the two interface fields
  \texttt{HeightData.spr\_padicBSD} (the norm identity) and
  \texttt{HeightData.spr\_nondeg} (the iff), both phrased over
  \texttt{HeightData}'s own proxies \texttt{c2norm}, \texttt{shaOrd} rather
  than \texttt{IsUnit (coeff 2 Lp)} or \texttt{Subsingleton ShaDual}. The
  proxies are the one deliberate exception to the rule that an interface
  field states its conclusion in the terms the theorems use; they are kept
  so that the anti-vacuity witness \texttt{ToySha}
  (Chapter~\ref{ch:nonvacuity}) stays constructible.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Analytic--arithmetic dictionary]
  \label{prop:dictionary}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.prop_dictionary}
  \leanok
  \uses{eq:padicbsd, def:c2tilde}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$, and let
  $p \notin S_E$ be a split prime of good reduction. The following are
  equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$;
    \item the cyclotomic $p$-adic height pairing on $E(\Q)$ is nondegenerate
      with $v_p(\Reg_\gamma) = 0$, and $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Both directions are a single rewrite along three definitional
  tie-equations of \texttt{PrimeData}: \texttt{c2norm\_tie} identifies the
  height-side proxy \texttt{c2norm} with the normalised analytic jet
  $\tilde c_2(p)$; the interface iff \texttt{HeightData.spr\_nondeg}
  (Assumption~\ref{eq:padicbsd}) supplies the equivalence in the proxy's own
  terms; \texttt{shaOrd\_tie} identifies the $\Sha$-order proxy being a unit
  with \texttt{Subsingleton ShaDual}. No non-anomality hypothesis is needed:
  unlike Theorem~\ref{prop:consequence}, this route never converts between
  $\tilde c_2$ and $c_2$.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The withdrawn anchor corollaries}
\label{ch:anchors}

Paper v1 carried two unconditional corollaries at the anchor primes
$p = 5$ and $p = 13$: $\Sha(E/\Q)[5^\infty] = 0$ and
$\Sha(E/\Q)[13^\infty] = 0$ for the testbed curve, each with the
accompanying corank, $\mu$, $\lambda$ and height-nondegeneracy conclusions.
They were derived from Theorem~\ref{prop:consequence} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:dictionary} by feeding in machine-verified $p$-adic
expansions of $c_2(p)$, carried as fields of a structure
\texttt{Certificates}.

Both were withdrawn from paper v2 on 2026-08-25. The same vanishing was
already in the literature: Coates, Liang and Sujatha proved
$\Sha(E_1/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$ for $E_1 : y^2 = x^3 + 14x$, the
$2$-isogenous partner of $E$, at every prime $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ with
$p < 30{,}000$, and the two groups differ by a $2$-isogeny, so the
corollaries were not new. On 2026-08-29 their Lean counterparts ---
\texttt{cor\_sha5},
\texttt{cor\_sha13}, the \texttt{Certificates} structure and the four
digit-certificate extraction lemmas --- were moved to
\texttt{legacy/Anchors.lean}, which is gitignored and outside the build and
the axiom audit. Nothing in the current formalization depends on them.

This chapter is kept, without theorem-like environments, so that the
withdrawal is visible rather than inferred from a gap. The numerical $c_2$
data survive in one place only: \texttt{toySha\_fails\_c2\_5\_certificate}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:nonvacuity}), which uses the seven computed $5$-adic digits
of $c_2(5)$ to exclude the anti-vacuity world. Paper v2 displays those
digits nowhere, so that lemma has no paper cross-reference.

\chapter{Towards \texorpdfstring{$\delta_E$}{delta\_E}: the Eisenstein--Kronecker mechanism}

\begin{assumption}[The central second jet as a second moment]
  \label{prop:jetformula}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.KatzData.grading_congr}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$, and let
  $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ be a prime of good reduction split in $K$. Let
  $L^{\mathrm{K}}(T) \in W[[T]]$ be the restriction of the Katz measure of
  $E$, twisted to the central character of $\psi_E$, to the cyclotomic line,
  and let $\ell$ be the composite of the cyclotomic character with $\log_p$.
  Then $\int d\mu_\psi = \int \ell\, d\mu_\psi = 0$ and
  \[
    c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}}) = \frac{1}{2\log_p(1+p)^2}\int_{G_\infty} \ell(g)^2\, d\mu_\psi(g) .
  \]

  \textbf{Descope.} The integral formula is not carried in Lean; only the
  algebraic congruence it feeds into (Proposition~\ref{prop:grading}(1)) is
  present, as the interface field \texttt{KatzData.grading\_congr}:
  $\exists\, \kappa_0 \in W^\times,\ p^2\bigl(\kappa_0 \cdot
  \mathrm{coeff}_2\,L^{\mathrm{Katz}} - \texttt{m2core}\bigr) \in (p^3)$.
  Formalizing the measure-theoretic integral is out of scope for this
  project. SOURCE: Bannai--Kobayashi, Duke Math. J. 153 (2010),
  Prop.~3.3/Thm.~3.7, plus de~Shalit II \S4.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Grading of the second moment; the criterion class]
  \label{prop:grading}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.isUnit_iff_residue_ne_zero_of_grading_congr}
  \uses{prop:jetformula}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$, let $\ff$ be the conductor
  of $\psi_E$, and let $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ be a prime of good reduction
  split in $K$ with $p \nmid \#\mathrm{Cl}_\ff(K)$. Write
  $G_\infty = \Delta \times G^{(1)}$; for $\delta \in \Delta$ let
  $\mu_\delta$ be the restriction of $\mu_\psi$ to the coset
  $\delta \times G^{(1)}$, in coordinates $(x,y)$ on $(1+p\Z_p)^2$; put
  $u_1 = x - 1$, $u_2 = y - 1$, and set
  $M_2(\fp) := \sum_{\delta \in \Delta} \int (u_1 + u_2)^2\, d\mu_\delta$.
  Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $2\log_p(1+p)^2\, c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}}) \equiv M_2(\fp)
      \pmod{p^3 W}$;
    \item $v_p(M_2(\fp)) \ge 2$, and $v_p(c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}})) = 0$ if and
      only if the \emph{criterion class}
      $\mathfrak{c}(\fp) := p^{-2}M_2(\fp) \bmod \mathfrak{m}_W$ is nonzero.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Part (1) is the classical premise, carried as the interface field
\texttt{KatzData.grading\_congr} of Assumption~\ref{prop:jetformula} and not
independently proved here; the node therefore carries no
\texttt{\textbackslash leanok}, so as not to overclaim it. Part (2) is proved
unconditionally, as an abstract local-domain valuation criterion with no
interface dependence: given a local integral domain $W$ with maximal ideal
$(\varpi)$, a unit $\kappa_0$, and $c, m \in W$ with
$\varpi^2(\kappa_0 c - m) \in (\varpi^3)$, then
$\mathrm{IsUnit}\, c \iff \mathrm{residue}(m) \ne 0$. That is the kernel
lemma named above, sorry-free and mathlib-only, instantiated at
$\varpi := p$ inside \texttt{thm\_reduction}.

\begin{lemma}[Decoupling]
  \label{lem:decoupling}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.Decoupling.coeff_two_mul, FinShaRank2.Decoupling.isUnit_coeff_two_of_comparison}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:comparison}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $A, B \in R[[T]]$ satisfy
      $c_0(B) = c_1(B) = 0$. Then $c_2(AB) = A(0)\, c_2(B)$.
    \item Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary
      quadratic field $K$, with $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$, and let
      $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ be a prime of good reduction split in $K$ with
      $p \notin S_E$. Then $v_p(c_2(p)) = v_p(c_2(L^{\mathrm{K}}))$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Part (1) is \texttt{Decoupling.coeff\_two\_mul}: $c_2(AB) = c_0(A)c_2(B) +
  c_1(A)c_1(B) + c_2(A)c_0(B)$ and the last two terms vanish. It is
  ring-generic, with no interface dependence.

  Part (2) is rendered as the transfer of unit-ness rather than as the
  equality of valuations: \texttt{Decoupling.isUnit\_coeff\_two\_of\_comparison}
  applies part (1) along a local ring hom to move
  $\mathrm{IsUnit}(\mathrm{coeff}_2\, L^{\mathrm{Katz}})$ across the
  comparison of Assumption~\ref{lem:comparison} to
  $\mathrm{IsUnit}(\mathrm{coeff}_2\, L_p)$. That direction is the one
  \texttt{thm\_reduction} uses.

  What part (1) rules out is a contribution to the leading grade from any
  derivative of the comparison or Euler-type factors, and in particular a
  Fermat-quotient term. The paper makes that point in the remark
  \texttt{rmk:fq} after the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[The divisor and the jet package]
  \label{eq:jetpackage}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.EKPackage}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, let $\ff$ be the conductor of its Grossencharacter $\psi_E$, let
  $\varepsilon$ be its unit character, fix a generator $f_0$ of $\ff$, and
  for $g \in (\OK/\ff)^\times$ put $t_g := g\,\Omega_\infty/f_0$. The divisor
  is the ray class group $D_E := \mathrm{Cl}_\ff(K) = (\OK/\ff)^\times/\mu_K$,
  and the package is the set of six functions on $D_E$
  \[
    \mathcal{R}_E := \Bigl\{ r_{a,b} \colon [g] \mapsto
      \varepsilon(g)^{-(a+b)}\, \frac{e^*_{a,b}(t_g, 0)}{A(\Gamma)^a}
      \;:\; a \ge 0,\ b \ge 1,\ a + b \le 3 \Bigr\},
  \]
  graded by $a + b - 1$, with values in $\Qbar$.

  \textbf{Classical input, not formalized as a theorem.} SOURCE:
  Bannai--Kobayashi, \emph{Algebraic theta functions and the $p$-adic
  interpolation of Eisenstein--Kronecker numbers}, Duke Math. J. 153 (2010),
  no. 2, 229--295, (15) and Thm.~1.17 for the coefficients $e^*_{a,b}$, and
  Thm.~2.9 with Cor.~2.11 for algebraicity of the values after division by
  $A(\Gamma)^a$; finiteness of $D_E$ is finiteness of the ray class group.
  Rendered in Lean as the structure \texttt{EKPackage}: the coefficient field
  \texttt{K}, the value field \texttt{L} (the paper's $\Qbar$), a finite
  nonempty \texttt{Finset} \texttt{D} of points, the six sections
  \texttt{r : Fin 6 \(\to\) \(\iota\) \(\to\) L}, a multiplicative
  \texttt{Valuation} \texttt{v p} on \texttt{L} for each rational prime $p$,
  and the support function \texttt{supp} of \texttt{hyp:sinnott}. The
  elliptic-curve construction behind those values is not unfolded; that is
  the descope recorded in the closing chapter.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[The invariants $\delta_E(c)$]
  \label{def:deltaE}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.EKPackage.deltaE, FinShaRank2.EKPackage.deltaE_ne_zero_iff, FinShaRank2.jetIndex_image, FinShaRank2.jetIndex_injective}
  \leanok
  \uses{eq:jetpackage}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, and let $\mathcal{R}_E$ and $D_E$ be the package and the divisor
  of Assumption~\ref{eq:jetpackage}. For $c = (c_{a,b}) \in K^6$, indexed by
  the six slots of $\mathcal{R}_E$, set
  \[
    F_c := \sum_{a,b} c_{a,b}\, r_{a,b} \colon D_E \longrightarrow \Qbar,
    \qquad
    \delta_E(c) := \mathrm{Res}(F_c, D_E) = \prod_{t \in D_E} F_c(t) .
  \]
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{EKPackage.Fc} and \texttt{EKPackage.deltaE} are definitions, not
  data: the product is written out, so \texttt{def:deltaE} is discharged by
  unfolding. The sentence the paper states just after the definition ---
  $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$ exactly when $F_c$ vanishes at no point of $D_E$ ---
  is the one-line consequence \texttt{deltaE\_ne\_zero\_iff}.
  \texttt{jetIndex} names which \texttt{Fin 6} slot is which pair $(a,b)$;
  \texttt{jetIndex\_image} checks by \texttt{decide} that the enumeration is
  exactly the paper's index set $\{(a,b) : b \ge 1,\ a + b \le 3\}$, and
  \texttt{jetIndex\_injective} that the six slots are six distinct pairs.

  \textbf{$\delta_E(c)$ lands in $\Qbar$, not in $K$.} $K$-rationality is
  Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit}(1), which rests on an equivariance input the paper
  uses rather than proves, and Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} does not use it.
  Accordingly the divisibility test $\fp \nmid \delta_E(c)$ is a valuation on
  \texttt{L}, not \texttt{padicValRat} on a rational number.

  Until 2026-08-29 this node was an assumption: $\delta_E$ was an opaque
  rational field \texttt{KatzData.deltaE\_local}, bundled with an assumed
  implication \texttt{KatzData.resultant\_link} from $\fp \nmid \delta_E$ to
  nonvanishing on $D_E$. Both fields are deleted; the content of the second
  is now the proved Lemma~\ref{lem:resultantstep}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Orbit criterion]
  \label{lem:orbit}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.Orbit.prod_mem_range_algebraMap, FinShaRank2.Orbit.forall_eq_zero_of_exists_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  Let $L/K$ be a Galois extension of fields, let $D$ be a finite nonempty
  set carrying an action of $\Gal(L/K)$, and let $F \colon D \to L$ satisfy
  $F(\sigma t) = \sigma(F(t))$ for all $\sigma \in \Gal(L/K)$ and $t \in D$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\prod_{t \in D} F(t) \in K$.
    \item If $\Gal(L/K)$ acts transitively on $D$, then $F$ vanishes either
      at every point of $D$ or at none.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Both parts are mathlib-only Galois theory over an abstract Galois
  extension and an abstract $\Gal(L/K)$-set, so instantiating at $D_E$ and
  $F_c$ is a substitution. Part (1): every $\sigma$ permutes $D$, so
  reindexing the product along \texttt{MulAction.toPerm \(\sigma\)} shows
  $\sigma$ fixes it, and
  \texttt{InfiniteGalois.mem\_range\_algebraMap\_iff\_fixed} puts it in the
  image of $K$. The conclusion is stated as membership of
  \texttt{Set.range (algebraMap K L)} rather than of an
  \texttt{IntermediateField}, which avoids subtype coercions at the call
  site, and uses the \texttt{InfiniteGalois} form because the
  \texttt{IsGalois} form additionally requires
  \texttt{[FiniteDimensional K L]}, which the lemma does not assume. Part
  (2): transitivity gives $\sigma$ with $t = \sigma t_0$, and equivariance
  gives $F(t) = \sigma(F(t_0)) = 0$; neither finiteness of $D$ nor the
  Galois hypothesis is used.

  This lemma is a faithful rendering of a paper lemma, not a link in the
  main chain. The paper applies it granting an input it does not prove ---
  the reciprocity $r_{a,b}(\sigma t) = \sigma(r_{a,b}(t))$, which is the
  hypothesis \texttt{hF} here --- and records that
  Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} does not depend on it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Unit factors of a unit resultant]
  \label{lem:resultantstep}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.Resultant.forall_eq_one_of_prod_eq_one, FinShaRank2.Resultant.prod_ne_zero_of_prod_eq_one}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:deltaE}
  Let $v$ be a valuation on a field $L$, let $D$ be a finite index set, and
  let $F \colon D \to L$. If $v(F(t)) \ge 0$ for every $t \in D$ and
  $v\bigl(\prod_{t \in D} F(t)\bigr) = 0$, then $v(F(t)) = 0$ for every
  $t \in D$. If moreover the value group is nontrivial, then
  $\prod_{t \in D} F(t) \ne 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  This is the valuation step in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}:
  by \texttt{hyp:sinnott}(i) each factor $F_c(t)$ of
  $\delta_E(c) = \prod_{t \in D_E} F_c(t)$ is $\fp$-integral, so
  $v_\fp(\delta_E(c))$ is a sum of nonnegative terms, and
  $\fp \nmid \delta_E(c)$ forces $v_\fp(F_c(t)) = 0$ at every $t$. The Lean
  proof distributes $v$ over the product (\texttt{map\_prod}, available
  because \texttt{Valuation} is a \texttt{MonoidWithZeroHomClass}) and
  applies \texttt{Finset.prod\_eq\_one\_iff\_of\_le\_one'}.

  \textbf{Sign convention.} Mathlib's \texttt{Valuation} is multiplicative,
  so the paper's $v_\fp(x) \ge 0$ is \texttt{v x \(\le\) 1} and the paper's
  $v_\fp(x) = 0$ is \texttt{v x = 1}. The multiplicative encoding is forced
  by the pin: \texttt{AddValuation} carries no lemma for the valuation of a
  product, and $\delta_E(c)$ is a product.

  \texttt{prod\_ne\_zero\_of\_prod\_eq\_one} is a small strengthening over
  the paper, which carries $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$ as a separate hypothesis of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}: at the point where the valuation argument is
  made, that hypothesis follows from $\fp \nmid \delta_E(c)$. It does not
  make the paper's hypothesis redundant, because \texttt{thm:reduction} also
  uses $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$ outside this step --- to witness the single
  $\delta_E$ that Conjecture~\ref{conj:strong} asserts works at every prime
  at once.

  Until 2026-08-29 this step was the assumed \texttt{KatzData} field
  \texttt{resultant\_link}, which asserted its conclusion outright; the
  integrality it rested on is now the hypothesis
  \texttt{SinnottHyp.integral}, where the paper puts it.
\end{proof}

\begin{hypothesis}[Sinnott trace property for second jets --- CONJECTURAL]
  \label{hyp:sinnott}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.SinnottHyp}
  \uses{prop:grading, def:deltaE}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$; let $\mathcal{R}_E$, $D_E$
  and $F_c$ be as in Assumption~\ref{eq:jetpackage} and
  Definition~\ref{def:deltaE}, and let $\mathfrak{c}(\fp)$ be the criterion
  class of Proposition~\ref{prop:grading}. For $c \in K^6$ write
  $\operatorname{supp}(c)$ for the finite set of rational primes lying below
  a prime of $\Qbar$ at which some entry of $c$ is not a unit.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] (\emph{Presentation.}) There exist a nonzero $c \in K^6$ and
      an integer $k \ge 1$, both independent of $p$, such that for every
      prime $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ of good reduction split in $K$ with
      $p \notin S_E \cup \operatorname{supp}(c)$: the values $F_c(t)$,
      $t \in D_E$, and their $\fp^k$-torsion translates are $\fp$-integral,
      and $\mathfrak{c}(\fp) = \sum_{t \in D_E} \sum_s w_{t,s}\,
      \overline{F_c}(t \oplus s)$, where $s$ runs over the $\fp^k$-torsion
      translation data and the weights $w_{t,s}$ are units of $W$.
    \item[(ii)] (\emph{Nonvanishing.}) For every presentation as in (i) and
      every such $p$: if $v_\fp(F_c(t)) = 0$ for all $t \in D_E$, then the
      sum displayed in (i) is nonzero.
  \end{enumerate}

  \textbf{One of the project's two conjectural inputs} (the other is
  \texttt{conj:EK} below). \texttt{SinnottHyp} is a \texttt{Prop}-valued
  structure with three fields: \texttt{integral} (the $\fp$-integrality of
  (i), as \texttt{P.v p (P.Fc c t) \(\le\) 1} for every $t \in D_E$),
  \texttt{presentation} (the displayed identity, as the equality of the
  residue of \texttt{m2core} with the opaque \texttt{traceClass}), and
  \texttt{nonvanishing} (the implication of (ii), whose antecedent is the
  paper's explicit pointwise condition \texttt{P.v p (P.Fc c t) = 1}). It
  occurs only in hypothesis position, in \texttt{thm\_reduction} and
  \texttt{thm\_reduction\_of\_conjEK}, and is never a field of any
  instantiable structure, so the assumption surface
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs} stays free of conjecture.

  Until 2026-08-29 the integrality clause sat in \texttt{KatzData}, on the
  instantiable surface, and the nonvanishing antecedent was an opaque
  \texttt{Prop}. Both now read as the paper states them.

  Part (i) is bookkeeping in the Bannai--Kobayashi calculus, which the paper
  expects to be provable but has not carried to the last constant; part (ii)
  is the jet-level analogue of Sinnott's lemma, and the paper records that
  nothing in the literature settles it.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{conjecture}[Eisenstein--Kronecker nonvanishing --- CONJECTURAL]
  \label{conj:EK}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ConjEK}
  \uses{def:deltaE}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $L(E,1) = 0$, $w(E) = +1$ and $\#\mathrm{Cl}_\ff(K) \ge 6$,
  and let $\delta_E(c)$ be the invariants of Definition~\ref{def:deltaE}.
  Then $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$ for every nonzero $c \in K^6$.

  \textbf{The project's other conjectural input.} \texttt{ConjEK H} renders
  the conjecture as a statement about the package \texttt{H.ek} alone:
  \texttt{\(\forall\) c : Fin 6 \(\to\) H.ek.K, c \(\ne\) 0 \(\to\)
  H.ek.deltaE c \(\ne\) 0}. It occurs as a code term at its own definition
  and in the hypotheses of \texttt{thm\_reduction\_of\_conjEK}, and nowhere
  else; it is not a field of any structure. \texttt{thm\_reduction} takes
  the single instance $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$ instead.

  \textbf{Descope: $\#\mathrm{Cl}_\ff(K) \ge 6$ is not rendered.} The paper
  carries the hypothesis and shows it is forced --- the equivariant
  functions on the torsor $D_E$ form a $K$-space of dimension
  $\#\mathrm{Cl}_\ff(K)$, so with fewer classes than sections some
  combination vanishes identically on $D_E$. In this encoding
  \texttt{EKPackage.D} is an abstract \texttt{Finset} with no class group and
  no equivariance, so the hypothesis has nothing to attach to. Dropping it
  widens what \texttt{ConjEK} asserts: a package with $\#D < 6$ makes
  \texttt{ConjEK} false, and nothing in the encoding rules such a package
  out. \texttt{ConjEK H} therefore renders \texttt{conj:EK} only for those
  \texttt{H} whose package meets the paper's hypothesis. This node carries no
  \texttt{\textbackslash leanok} for that reason: the Lean statement does not match the
  paper's on the nose, where \texttt{ConjStrong} and \texttt{ConjWeak}
  (Conjectures~\ref{conj:strong} and~\ref{conj:weak}) do.
\end{conjecture}

\chapter{The reduction theorem}
\label{ch:reduction}

\begin{theorem}[Reduction; $=$ Theorem C]
  \label{thm:reduction}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.thm_reduction, FinShaRank2.thm_reduction_of_conjEK}
  \leanok
  \uses{prop:consequence, lem:decoupling, prop:grading, hyp:sinnott, conj:EK, prop:normalisation, lem:comparison, lem:resultantstep, def:deltaE, conj:strong}
  Let $E/\Q$ have CM by the maximal order $\OK$ of an imaginary quadratic
  field $K$, with $\rank E(\Q) = 2$, $L(E,1) = 0$ and $w(E) = +1$. Assume
  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:sinnott} for $E$, let $c$ be a vector as in its part
  (i), and assume $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$. Then for every prime
  $p = \fp\bar\fp \ge 5$ of good reduction split in $K$ with
  $p \notin S_E \cup \operatorname{supp}(c)$ and $\fp \nmid \delta_E(c)$,
  \[
    \tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times, \qquad
    \lambda_{\mathrm{an}}(\fp) = 2, \qquad
    \Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0 .
  \]
  If moreover $S_E$ is finite, then Conjecture~\ref{conj:strong} holds for
  $E$ with $\delta_E = \delta_E(c)$ (Definition~\ref{def:deltaE}) and with
  $\Sigma = S_E \cup \operatorname{supp}(c)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \textbf{Conjectural input appears in this chapter and nowhere else}, and
  strictly in hypothesis position: \texttt{hEK : H.ek.deltaE c \(\ne\) 0},
  one instance of Conjecture~\ref{conj:EK}, and \texttt{hsin}, which is
  \texttt{SinnottHyp} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:sinnott}) quantified over every
  split $p \notin H.S \cup \texttt{H.ek.supp c}$.

  \textbf{Assembly route}: \texttt{hsin.integral} makes every factor of
  $\delta_E(c) = \prod_{t \in D_E} F_c(t)$ $\fp$-integral, and with
  $\fp \nmid \delta_E(c)$ Lemma~\ref{lem:resultantstep} gives
  $v_\fp(F_c(t)) = 0$ at every $t \in D_E$. That is the antecedent of
  \texttt{hsin.nonvanishing}, which yields $\mathrm{traceClass} \ne 0$;
  \texttt{hsin.presentation} identifies this with the residue of
  \texttt{m2core}; the kernel lemma of Proposition~\ref{prop:grading}(2)
  turns that into $\mathrm{IsUnit}(\mathrm{coeff}_2\, L^{\mathrm{Katz}})$;
  the comparison of Assumption~\ref{lem:comparison}, transferred by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:decoupling}'s kernel lemma, moves unit-ness to
  $\mathrm{coeff}_2\, L_p$; Proposition~\ref{prop:normalisation}, fed
  non-anomality by \texttt{ClassicalInputs.isPUnit\_one\_sub\_alphaInv},
  converts this to $\tilde c_2(p) \in \Z_p^\times$; and
  Theorem~\ref{prop:consequence} supplies the remaining conclusions. Every
  step downstream of \texttt{hEK}/\texttt{hsin} is sorry-free; nothing about
  the conjectural hypotheses is discharged, only consumed.

  \texttt{hEK} is what witnesses the $\delta \ne 0$ conjunct of
  \texttt{ConjStrong H}. At an individual prime it would be free, by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:resultantstep}; but Conjecture~\ref{conj:strong} asserts a
  single nonzero $\delta_E$ valid at every prime at once, and that is what
  \texttt{hEK} supplies.

  \texttt{thm\_reduction\_of\_conjEK} is the same statement with
  \texttt{hEK} replaced by \texttt{ConjEK H} and the nonzeroness
  \texttt{hc : c \(\ne\) 0} of the vector, which is what
  \texttt{hyp:sinnott}(i) asserts of the vector it produces. It renders the
  paper's parenthetical that input (i) of Theorem~C follows from
  \texttt{conj:EK}. It is the only declaration in the project that mentions
  \texttt{ConjEK}.

  \textbf{Three points of shape.} The paper's last assertion is conditional
  on $S_E$ being finite; \texttt{H.S} is a \texttt{Finset}, so the condition
  holds by construction and disappears. The per-prime conclusion reports
  \texttt{MuZero} alongside $\lambda_{\mathrm{an}} = 2$, the two being
  paired throughout because \texttt{lambdaAn} is junk-valued without it. And
  \texttt{ConjStrong} omits the integrality clause of
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:strong}, for the reason given there.

  Until 2026-08-29 this theorem also carried the residual arithmetic
  hypothesis $p = 5 \Rightarrow a_p = -2$; the interface field
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs.notAnomalous} removed it.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Non-vacuity of the assumption surface}
\label{ch:nonvacuity}

\begin{theorem}[The assumption surface is satisfiable]
  \label{nonvac:trivial}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ToyTrivial}
  \leanok
  There is a proved instance \texttt{ToyTrivial : ClassicalInputs}, with
  every field discharged in a toy world ($S = \varnothing$, $D_E$ a single
  point, $L_p = X^2$, \ldots) and no incomplete proofs. So
  \texttt{ClassicalInputs} is not internally contradictory, and the headline
  theorems are not vacuously true.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  Every one of the interface layers (\texttt{AnalyticData},
  \texttt{IwasawaData}/\texttt{SelmerData}, \texttt{HeightData},
  \texttt{KatzData}, \texttt{EKPackage}) is instantiated concretely
  (\texttt{Toy/Analytic.lean}, \texttt{Toy/Iwasawa.lean},
  \texttt{Toy/Heights.lean}, \texttt{Toy/Katz.lean}, \texttt{Toy/EK.lean})
  and the three tie-equations of \texttt{PrimeData} are discharged by direct
  computation. \texttt{ClassicalInputs.notAnomalous} is discharged from the
  existing toy data through \texttt{Toy.Setup.ap\_ne\_one}; no toy datum was
  changed to fit it. \texttt{\#print axioms ToyTrivial} shows only
  \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}.

  \texttt{Toy.toyEK} is the shared Eisenstein--Kronecker package of both toy
  instances: a one-point divisor over $\Q$, with $F_c$ the sum of the six
  coefficients, so $\delta_E(c) \ne 0$ reduces to $\sum_i c_i \ne 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The assumption surface does not entail the conclusion]
  \label{nonvac:sha}
  \lean{FinShaRank2.ToySha, FinShaRank2.interface_does_not_force_sha_trivial, FinShaRank2.toySha_conclusions_fail, FinShaRank2.toySha_fails_c2_5_certificate}
  \leanok
  The interface \texttt{ClassicalInputs} does \emph{not}, by itself, entail
  $\Sha(E/\Q)[p^\infty] = 0$: there is a second proved instance
  \texttt{ToySha : ClassicalInputs} at which $\Sha[p^\infty] \ne 0$,
  $\mu_{\mathrm{an}} \ne 0$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{an}} \ne 2$, and at which
  the computed $5$-adic expansion of $c_2(5)$ is false. So the numerical
  $c_2$ datum, not the interface, is what excludes this world.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean]
  \leanok
  \texttt{ToySha : ClassicalInputs} (\texttt{FinShaRank2/Toy/ShaTrivial.lean})
  instantiates the interface layers in a second toy world, distinct from
  \texttt{ToyTrivial}, at which $\Sha$ is nontrivial.
  \texttt{toySha\_conclusions\_fail} shows three of the headline conclusions
  --- $\Sha[p^\infty] = 0$, $\mu_{\mathrm{an}} = 0$,
  $\lambda_{\mathrm{an}} = 2$ --- fail at \emph{every} split prime of this
  world, not merely at some exceptional prime.
  \texttt{interface\_does\_not\_force\_sha\_trivial} packages this as the
  non-entailment statement: \texttt{ClassicalInputs} alone does not force
  $\Sha = 0$.

  \texttt{toySha\_fails\_c2\_5\_certificate} identifies the datum that does.
  In this world $\mathrm{coeff}_2\, L_p = p$, so at $p = 5$ the leading
  $5$-adic digit of $c_2(5)$ is $0$; this project's computation of $c_2(5)$
  for the testbed curve has leading digit $1$, and the lemma proves the
  seven-digit congruence fails. Removing the numerical datum therefore does
  not merely weaken the theorem: it admits a world in which the headline
  conclusions are false.

  Until 2026-08-29 this role was played by
  \texttt{isEmpty\_certificates\_toySha}, which proved that
  \texttt{Certificates ToySha} is empty. That structure was retired with the
  anchor corollaries (Chapter~\ref{ch:anchors}) and the certificate-free
  lemma above replaced it, carrying the same content.

  \textbf{No paper cross-reference.} The seven $5$-adic digits appear in
  this lemma and nowhere else in the project. Paper v1 displayed them; paper
  v2 dropped the display together with the anchor corollaries it served, so
  a referee will find no paper statement to check them against.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Descoped material: not formalized}
\label{ch:descoped}

The items below are genuine content of the paper that this formalization
deliberately does not attempt. None of them carries a \texttt{\textbackslash lean} tag or a
\texttt{\textbackslash leanok} mark, and none of them is a node the proved theorems above
\texttt{\textbackslash uses}: their absence from the dependency graph is intentional, not an
oversight. Full prose documentation of each is in
\texttt{FORMALIZATION.md}; this chapter exists so a referee scanning the
blueprint sees the boundary explicitly rather than inferring it from
silence.

\paragraph{Expository background (\texttt{thm:gillard}, (BK1)--(BK3)).}
The Gillard/Sinnott theorem that the $\mu$-invariant of every branch of the
Katz measure vanishes --- the classical \emph{zeroth}-jet analogue of this
paper's second-jet question --- and the three foundational
Bannai--Kobayashi facts about the Kronecker theta function (generating
function, algebraicity, $p$-adic interpolation at ordinary primes) that
\S\texttt{sec:mechanism} recalls as background. Purely expository; nothing
downstream depends on a Lean rendering of these.

\paragraph{Failure modes and commentary
(\texttt{ssec:failuremodes}, \texttt{rmk:fq}, \texttt{rmk:modesnow},
\texttt{rmk:correlation}).} The section listing the ways
Conjecture~\ref{conj:strong} could fail (irreducible $\fp$-dependence,
after the pattern of the Kubota--Leopoldt functions, where the horizontal
$\lambda$-statement is known to be \emph{false}; Wieferich-type collapse of
$\delta_E$); the remark on why Fermat-quotient terms are structurally
absent from the leading grade at a double zero; the remark identifying
\texttt{hyp:sinnott} and \texttt{conj:EK} as the formal loci of those
failure modes; and the remark on how the regulator scan acquires algebraic
meaning through \texttt{thm:reduction}. All are mathematical commentary and
motivation, not statements this project proves or assumes as Lean data.

\paragraph{The regulator scan (\texttt{ssec:scan},
\texttt{ssec:scanweight}).} The $508$-prime computational scan testing
$v_\fp(\Reg_\fp) = 2$ against a null model, and its statistical analysis.
Computational, not formalized. The scripts that produce it are in
\texttt{code/}, not in the Lean tree.

\paragraph{The Coates--Liang--Sujatha input (\texttt{thm:cls},
\texttt{lem:isogeny}, \texttt{cor:shavanishing}).} The vanishing of
$\Sha[p^\infty]$ for the $2$-isogenous partner of the testbed curve at every
split $p < 30{,}000$, and its transfer along the isogeny. Cited from the
literature, and the reason the two anchor corollaries were withdrawn
(Chapter~\ref{ch:anchors}); not formalized.

\paragraph{Open problems and outlook (\texttt{sec:outlook}).} Prospective
discussion of contrasts with Rubin's conjecture on local units, Rubin's
inert-prime $p$-adic Gross--Zagier analysis, and other open directions.
Prospective by nature; not a target for formalization.

\paragraph{Geometric and analytic content left inside interface fields.}
Beyond \texttt{prop:jetformula}'s integral formula
(Assumption~\ref{prop:jetformula}), the construction of the divisor $D_E$
and of the sections $r_{a,b}$ (Assumption~\ref{eq:jetpackage}) and the
$p$-adic height-pairing analytics behind Assumption~\ref{eq:padicbsd} are
consumed as opaque data, never unfolded into their underlying
analytic/geometric construction. This is by design: the referee checks
these field \emph{statements} against the cited literature, and Lean's
kernel certifies everything built on top of them.

\paragraph{Part (1) of \texttt{lem:noanomalous}.} That $p \not\equiv 1
\pmod 4$ implies $p \mid a_p$. Its proof reduces to Deuring's reduction
criterion, and mathlib has neither that criterion nor the reduction of an
elliptic curve with complex multiplication. Every downstream theorem uses
the split case, Lemma~\ref{lem:noanomalous}, only.
